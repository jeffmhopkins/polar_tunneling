Polar Tunneling: methods and results

A batter's-eye pitch model built from public Statcast data, a pitch sequencer built on it, and the pre-registered tests run on both. Every model and run value was fit on the 2025 regular season and scored once on 2026. Numbers are taken from the reports in model/.

Summary

1. A per-pitcher Tunnel+ fails in both forms, geometric and perceptual. The pitcher-level values are stable but don't predict out-of-half results.

2. The commit-point features improve 2026 pitch-level log-loss over a full set of controls (whiff|swing +1.83, swing +5.86 per 1000). The gain survives speed controls.

3. As a pitch picker, the model beats FanGraphs Stuff+, Pitching+ and PitchingBot on CSW, and beats Pitching+ and PitchingBot on runs (about even with Stuff+).

4. It ties the same model without the perceptual features on pick quality (type, runs, type+zone). The features improve forecasts but rarely change the argmax.

Geometry

Constant-acceleration flight from the 9-parameter fit. The public CSV lacks x/z at y = 50 ft, so release state is recovered from plate_x/plate_z (the fit evaluated at the front of the plate, y = 17/12 ft). Time is absolute, measured back from arrival.

p(t) = p₀ + v₀·t + ½·a·t² v(t) = v₀ + a·t t_f : p_y(t_f) = 17/12 ft eye e = (∓35.8, −28.3, 65) in (− for RHH, + for LHH; Statcast catcher's-view axes) commit time t_c = t_f − τ_b angle θ(p₁, p₂) = arccos[ (p₁−e)·(p₂−e) / (|p₁−e| |p₂−e|) ] angular speed ω = |v − (v·û)û| / |p − e|, û = (p − e)/|p − e| (deg/s)

Hitter timing (P1)

r_b = mean(swing_length / bat_speed) over b's swings with bat_speed > 40 mph r̃_b = (n_b·r_b + 50·r̄) / (n_b + 50) shrink to league mean r̄, prior 50 swings τ_b = clip(175 ms · r̃_b / r̄, 125, 225) missing → 175 ms

Reference clouds, usage and fastball paths are computed per season from trajectories and labels only, no outcomes. The first geometric version (features.py) used a fixed τ = 175 ms and the pair separations θ at commit (d_dec_deg), physical separation at the 100 ms tracking limit (d_track_in) and at the plate (d_plate_in) against the previous pitch.

Outcome models

Learner
LightGBM, binary objective (regression for xwOBA | BIP): learning rate 0.05, 63 leaves, min_child_samples 200, feature_fraction 0.8, bagging 0.8 every round, 400 rounds, seed 7. Fixed across every test, no tuning on 2026. Categoricals native.
Targets
swing; whiff | swing; called strike | take; CSW per pitch; foul | contact; xwOBA | ball in play.
M0 controls
pitch type, stand, p_throws, velo, horizontal and vertical movement, spin, extension, release height and side, plate location (batter-oriented x, z normalised to the zone), balls, strikes, platoon; previous pitch type, result and location; fastball differentials (Δvelo, Δmovement x/z, Δrelease x/z); arm angle; batter whiff|swing and swing rates (leave-one-game-out, prior 100).
Speed controls
Added from the speed check on: plate speed, flight time, Δvelo and Δflight time vs the previous pitch, Δflight time vs the fastball.
Standard model (B)
M0 + speed controls. No P features.
Perceptual model (A)
Standard + P1–P6.
Stuff-only (C)
Pitch type, stand, p_throws, velo, movement, spin, extension, release, arm angle, fastball differentials. No location, count or context. A Stuff+-style baseline.
Hitter layer
Batter's 2025 whiff, swing and chase rates against fastball / breaking / offspeed classes (9 features), for hitters with ≥ 200 pitches seen.

Sequencer

For pitcher j, side, count c and previous pitch type q, each type k in his menu (usage ≥ 4%, ≥ 40 pitches to that side) is scored by placing up to 20 of his real pitches of type k into the context and averaging the model's predictions. Context: previous pitch = his mean pitch of type q, previous result = the most common one leading into c, average hitter, first time through.

CSW_score(k | j, side, c, q) = (1/N) Σᵢ f_CSW(x_i^k ; context) top pick = argmax_k E[RV] = P(take)·[P(cs)·RV(c,cs) + (1−P(cs))·RV(c,ball)] + P(swing)·[P(whiff)·RV(c,whiff) + (1−P(whiff))·( P(foul)·RV(c,foul) + (1−P(foul))·RV_bip(c, E[xwOBA]) )] RV(c, outcome) = mean 2025 delta_run_exp by count and outcome RV_bip(c, xwOBA) = 2025 linear fit of delta_run_exp on count dummies + xwOBA, balls in play runs pick = argmin_k E[RV]

Out-of-sample grids (sequencing.py --train-years 2025) take the model, previous-result lookup and batter medians from 2025. 2026 is used only for pitch shapes, locations and usage, never outcomes. The production pages retrain on 2025 + 2026.

2What we tested

Protocol

  • Each test has a model/PREREG_*.md committed before any outcome for it was computed. It fixes the features, controls, endpoints and pass bar. The 2026 test is run once, and all results are reported.
  • Forecast endpoint: ΔLL = 1000 · (LLM0 − LLM1) on 2026, mean binary log-loss.
  • Null: M1 refit with all P columns permuted within pitcher × pitch type in both train and test, 5 draws. This keeps each pitcher × type's feature distribution, so a pass means the signal is in pitch-to-pitch variation, not identity. Pass bar: ΔLL ≥ 1.0 and above all 5 nulls on whiff | swing or swing.
  • Picker endpoint: within-count matched-minus-unmatched gap on 2026, where "matched" means the thrown type equals the 2025-model pick:
G = Σ_c n_c·( ȳ_c,matched − ȳ_c,unmatched ) / Σ_c n_c y = CSW (pts) or realised RV (runs/100) picker A vs B: G_A − G_B on the same pitches 95% CI: pitcher-cluster bootstrap (resample pitchers with replacement; 2000 draws for validation, 1000 later, 500 for regressions)
  • Selection controls: within-OLS, CSWi = β·matchedi + γ′Xi + αpitcher×side×count, with X = batter 2025 CSW-against (overall and vs the pitch's class), outs, runners, inning band, platoon, previous pitch class and result. Plus an IPW gap from a propensity model for throwing the top pick.
  • At-bat: adherence = PA match share − the pitcher's usual match rate in those counts. yPA = β·adh + γ′X + αpitcher×side, reported as β × 0.25 (per +25 pts adherence).
  • Realised RV of a test pitch: non-contact outcomes from the 2025 RV(c, outcome) table; balls in play from RVbip at the pitch's actual xwOBA, so contact is valued by quality of contact, not where it landed.
  • Picker sample: 2026 pitches from 265 pitchers with ≥ 1,000 pitches, about 470k.

3How it compared

Pickers built from 2025 only, scored on the same 470,321 2026 pitches (FanGraphs pickers on the 425,045 where the pitcher has at least two graded types). RA, RB and RC pick by min E[RV] with the perceptual, standard and stuff-only feature sets. A picks by max CSW. D = best 2025 CSW type for the count. E = the pitcher's most-used 2026 type in that count (usage only, no outcomes). FS / FP / FB = highest 2025 FanGraphs grade in his menu. FanGraphs grades are season grades per pitch, not count-aware, so this tests "throw the best-graded pitch", not what those models were built to do.

4Limits

  • Picker tests are observational. Matched vs unmatched pitches differ in ways the controls can't fully capture (scouting, catcher, game plan). The after-what gap came in larger than the model predicted (+2.57 vs +1.39), which points to residual selection. Read +3.56 as an upper bound.
  • Type, not intent. Statcast records the landing spot, not the target. The zone test mixes choice with command.
  • One holdout season. Everything is 2025 → 2026 with one fixed hyperparameter set.
  • Contact quality is weakly modelled. 2026 R² for xwOBA | BIP is 0.032 (0.030 without P and hitter features). The runs pick gains mainly by weighting outcomes by run value.
  • Eye position is generic (65 in high, fixed offset). τ_b comes from bat tracking; the per-hitter fitted τ didn't replicate (r = 0.04).
  • Grid assumptions: average hitter, first time through, modal previous result. Pitchers with fewer than 1,000 pitches are excluded from season tests.
