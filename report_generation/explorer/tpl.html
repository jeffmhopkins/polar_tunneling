Hitter's eye

Where the ball appears to the hitter, in degrees from their line of sight to the release point. Every pitch drifts down and toward the plate because the ball drops below eye level and passes in front of the hitter. Rings mark the decision point. "Early flight" stops 50 ms after it; "To the plate" adds the strike zone (dashed), drawn at the front edge of the plate, where each pitch ends.

What the hitter sees at the decision

Snapshot

Balls are drawn at their true apparent size from the hitter's eye. The tail is the last 100 ms of flight; the arrow is where the ball is heading over the next 50 ms. The dashed cross is the primary fastball. With "Eyes on the fastball" the frame moves with the primary fastball, so every arrow is motion relative to it: what peels off the fastball's path on the hitter's retina. "Fixed gaze" holds the frame on the fastball's decision-point spot and shows each pitch's own motion.

Fastball read vs. where it actually ends up · catcher's view

Hollow ring: where the pitch would cross the front of the plate if, from the decision point on, it kept bending like the primary fastball, which is the hitter's default read. Filled ball: where it actually crosses. The line between them is how much the read is off.

Apparent speed is how fast the ball's direction from the eye is changing. Separating is how fast the angle between this pitch and the fastball is growing. The fastball read starts from the pitch's actual position and velocity at the decision point and applies the fastball's acceleration from there; across is + away from the hitter, up/down is − when the pitch finishes below the read, timing is + when it arrives later than the read expects. These are the optical-motion and fastball-prior-miss features from the perceptual model in model/perceptual.py, evaluated on the average pitch.

What's approximate

  • Average pitches, not real ones. Each path is built from a pitcher's season-average release point, acceleration, speed and plate location for that pitch type, so pitch-to-pitch spread and sequencing aren't shown.
  • Paths are Statcast's own fit. Each pitch uses Statcast's 9-parameter constant-acceleration fit (vx0, vy0, vz0, ax, ay, az), run back to the release point and forward to the front edge of the plate. The fit reproduces Statcast's reported release point to within 0.1 in. Constant acceleration is itself an approximation: real drag and Magnus force change slightly over the flight.
  • Fixed hitter's eye. 65 in high, 35.8 in off the plate's center and 28.3 in behind its back tip, as set in to_polar.py. Averages pool both batter sides; the RHH/LHH switch only moves the eye. Gaps are measured at the same time after release, so a slower pitch's lag shows up as angle, and that reads differently from each side of the plate. Lefties top the RHH leaderboard partly for this reason; switch to LHH to compare.
  • No names. The data has MLBAM IDs only; the Savant link opens the pitcher's page. "Throws" is inferred from release side.
  • Sample. Regular season 2025 from Mar 20 to Oct 1 (includes two Wild Card days, misses the Tokyo Series). Pitchers with 300+ pitches and pitch types with 25+ are shown.
